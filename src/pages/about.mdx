# Hello! 👋

I'm Reece, a Lead Software Engineer at [CalcAir](https://calcairebs.com/), where I lead the move of one of our core products from the desktop to the cloud. I discovered coding at age 9 and never looked back, and I finished my B.S. in Computer Science with a minor in Mathematics at the University of Minnesota in three years.

# What I Work On

Most of my work sits where old systems meet new ones: modernizing long-lived desktop software, building the platforms and tooling other engineers build on, and moving systems to the cloud without disrupting the people who depend on them. I care as much about how a team builds as about what it builds, so I've led our adoption of Claude, Claude Code, and Cursor, introduced an AI-driven development lifecycle, and run workshops and monthly AI meetings to help other engineers get real results from these tools.

Outside of work, I build [pywire](/projects/pywire), an open-source Python web framework for interactive, server-rendered apps, along with its compiler, language server, and editor tooling.

# What Drives Me

I like solving hard problems, especially the ones where small details matter. Robotics, mathematics, AI, and simulations of reality are the things that make me tick. I'm inspired by technology that facilitates digital equity and empowers people to learn, grow, and succeed.

# Away From Keyboard

When I'm not at my computer, you might find me:

- 📝 Crossing national parks off the list
- ☕️ Refining my pourover coffee skills
- 🧗 Working on my projects at the climbing gym
- 🏔️ Planning my next adventure

Since January 2025, I've been a software mentor for the Eagan High School robotics team, FRC 2220. As a former team member, it's incredibly rewarding to help the next generation of engineers and problem solvers.

# Looking Forward 🚀

I want to keep growing as a technical leader: designing platforms that make other engineers faster, and figuring out what software development looks like when AI is a real part of the team. On the side, I'm working to take pywire to a stable 1.0.
