# About

I'm Reece, a Lead Software Engineer at [CalcAir](https://calcairebs.com/). I'm leading the move of one of our core products from a desktop app to the cloud. I started coding at 9 with a text adventure game in bash, then wrote a Minecraft Java mod. I finished a B.S. in Computer Science with a math minor at the University of Minnesota in three years.

# Work

I work where old software meets new. That means modernizing desktop software that's been around for decades, building the platforms and tooling other engineers build on, and getting systems to the cloud without disrupting the people who use them. I'm a generalist: frontend and design, backend, security, cloud infrastructure and internal tools all end up on my plate.

People at work come to me for institutional knowledge (I remember a lot), for a straight answer, and for what's current in software and AI. On the AI side, I run workshops and a monthly AI meeting, and I helped get Claude and Cursor adopted across our engineering team.

# pywire

[pywire](/projects/pywire) is my open-source Python web framework for interactive, server-rendered apps. It's also where I put what I think matters in web development and where I think the web is headed. It's the project I'm proudest of, and I'm working toward a stable 1.0.

# Simulation

I like making a simulation feel real. There's something special about running something on a computer to prepare for the real world. Physics engines are the most fun for me, because seeing math and physics principles play out on screen is a different experience than reading them off a page.

# FRC 2220

Since January 2025 I've been a software mentor for the Eagan High School robotics team, FRC 2220, where I was a student. I teach Java and Kotlin, though that isn't my specialty. I push the more senior students toward harder topics like control theory, task automation, computer vision, decision-making systems, testability and simulation. It pushes me too, and it gives the youngest students some direction in a confusing time to be starting out in software.

# Outside of Work

I climb, and I make espresso or pourover almost every day. I like traveling off the beaten path, in the US and abroad, as cheaply as I can.
